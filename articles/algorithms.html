What Is an Algorithm?

An algorithm is a repeatable procedure for turning an input into an output. It is the idea underneath search, maps, recommendations, and much of computing.

Think recipe, not magic

A recipe tells you what to do with ingredients to get a meal. An algorithm tells a computer what steps to perform on data to get a result. The important part is that the steps are precise enough to repeat.

Example: finding a name

If a list is sorted alphabetically, you don't need to check every name. You can look near the middle, decide whether the target is before or after it, and throw away half the remaining possibilities. Repeat.

One idea, huge consequence

Good algorithms often reduce the amount of work required. Computer science studies how to make those procedures correct, efficient, and useful.

Algorithms are everywhere

Your navigation app needs algorithms to find routes. A search engine needs algorithms to rank results. A recommendation system needs algorithms to choose what appears next. Machine-learning models are also algorithms—but they learn parameters from data rather than relying only on hand-written rules.
