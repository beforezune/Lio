Why ChatGPT Can Predict Words

The “next word” idea sounds simple. The interesting part is what a large neural network can learn while getting very, very good at it.

Start with a sentence

Give a model: “The capital of France is …” A useful language model should assign a high probability to “Paris”. During training, it sees enormous numbers of text examples and learns patterns that help it predict what comes next.

It isn't a lookup table

A modern language model doesn't simply store a phrase and retrieve the answer. Its parameters encode statistical patterns learned from training. Context changes the probabilities: “bank” after “river” behaves differently from “bank” after “money”.

Prediction is the training game

Context → model → probability distribution → predicted token. Compare with the real next token → calculate error → update parameters → repeat.

Where transformers enter

Transformers provide a powerful architecture for processing relationships between tokens. Their attention mechanism helps the model decide which parts of the context are useful when constructing its internal representation.

One important correction

“It predicts the next token” does not mean “it only knows how to autocomplete.” The behavior we see emerges from scale, architecture, training data, optimization, and post-training. Next-token prediction is the training objective—not a complete description of everything the finished system can do.
